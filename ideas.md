# Viral Delivery 3D — Design Direction

## Three possible directions

### Theme Name: Kinetic Street Signal
Very Brief Intro: A high-energy neo-brutalist delivery identity built from black, acid lime, and signal orange. Oversized type, tilted layers, and motion make the service feel made for the feed.
Probability: 0.07

### Theme Name: Soft City Courier
Very Brief Intro: A warm editorial system using cream, cobalt, and tomato red with hand-drawn route marks and tactile paper texture. It makes delivery feel human, local, and considered.
Probability: 0.04

### Theme Name: Midnight Orbit
Very Brief Intro: A dark atmospheric route system with chrome accents, electric blue navigation lines, and floating 3D courier artifacts. It feels premium, technical, and night-ready.
Probability: 0.09

## Selected Approach: Kinetic Street Signal

### Design Movement
Neo-brutalism crossed with contemporary sportswear/editorial art direction: hard edges, compressed typography, high-contrast fields, raw labels, and layered motion.

### Core Principles
1. Make the first screen instantly legible: delivery promise, service area, and action are visible before the user scrolls.
2. Use asymmetry and depth rather than a centered template: off-axis content, floating cards, and oversized numerals create energy.
3. Treat motion as brand language: parallax is physical, fast, and slightly playful, never decorative noise.
4. Keep every visual accent functional: labels, route marks, status chips, and offer strips help users scan and act.

### Color Philosophy
Ink black creates a confident stage for vivid signals. Acid lime marks freshness, speed, and the “now” moment; signal orange marks appetite, urgency, and the delivery offer. Warm off-white is reserved for readable editorial surfaces. The ownable signature color is **Route Lime #D7FF38**.

### Layout Paradigm
A vertical editorial route: hero → live delivery promise → service stories → operational proof → delivery offer → action footer. Sections alternate between full-bleed color fields and offset content rails. Key copy sits on a diagonal visual axis while supporting details anchor to a thin route line.

### Signature Elements
- A route-line motif with numbered stops, arrows, and small coordinate labels.
- Acid-lime “delivery ticket” cards that overlap dark surfaces.
- Huge outlined numerals and offset tags that create depth as they move with the pointer.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like a courier handoff: immediate, tactile, and directional. Buttons compress on press, cards tilt subtly toward the pointer, and the offer remains easy to find without blocking the page.

### Animation
Hero layers use pointer-based translate and rotate with restrained limits; background blobs drift slowly on the z-axis. On scroll, section labels and cards reveal with 30–70ms stagger. All transitions use transform and opacity only, with reduced-motion fallbacks that preserve hierarchy without movement.

### Typography System
Display: **Barlow Condensed**, 700–900, uppercase for headline impact and large numerals. Body: **DM Sans**, 400–700, for readable service descriptions and microcopy. Labels use Barlow Condensed at 11–13px with increased tracking. Headlines use tight line-height and intentional breaks.

### Brand Essence
For people and teams who need movement without friction, Viral Delivery turns local delivery into a fast, visible, memorable experience — direct, energetic, unmistakable. Personality: **fast, sharp, magnetic**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are short commands or confident declarations. CTAs sound like a next move, not a generic signup. Microcopy is useful, compact, and a little cheeky.

Example lines:
- “To order sou, to kinoume.”
- “Delivery pou fainetai prin ftasei.”

### Wordmark & Logo
A compact route-arrow symbol built from two offset chevrons inside a rounded-square ticket frame, paired with a condensed wordmark. The icon should work alone as a favicon and delivery-status marker.

### Signature Brand Color
**Route Lime — #D7FF38**

## Style Decisions

- The route-arrow ticket mark and condensed wordmark must be visible above the fold, not reserved for the footer.
- Every major section is treated as a numbered stop in one route spine, using dashed lines, arrows, dispatch labels, ETA chips, or handoff markers.
- Headlines, CTAs, and key labels use courier-command language: short, directional, and slightly cheeky.
- The hero promise explicitly states what moves, the local operating area, and the ETA before the visitor scrolls.
